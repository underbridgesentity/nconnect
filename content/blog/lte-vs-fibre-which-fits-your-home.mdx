The honest answer to "should I get LTE or fibre?" is: it depends on your
address, and anyone who answers without asking where you live is guessing.
Here's how we walk customers through it.

## Start with what reaches your house

Fibre is the better product *where it exists*: symmetrical speeds, no signal
variables, no per-gigabyte anxiety. But it only exists where an operator has
trenched your street. LTE and 5G reach almost everywhere the networks have
towers, which is most of the country.

So the first step is never a product comparison; it's a coverage check. Do
that [here](/coverage), fibre answers come back within one business day.

## The cost picture

At the entry level the two are closer than people expect: our 30/50Mbps fibre
starts at R533, while uncapped LTE starts at R388 plus a once-off router.
Over a year, LTE's total cost is usually lower; fibre wins on cost-per-Mbps
as you climb the speed ladder.

## Load-shedding changes the maths

An LTE router on a small DC UPS (around R666) rides through a stage-4 slot
easily. Fibre needs both your router *and* the operator's street equipment to
have power, some areas hold up well, others drop. Neighbours with fibre are
your best data source; ask them what stage kills their line.

## When we recommend what

- **Fibre**, if it's live in your street and you stream, game, or work on
  video calls daily. It's the calmer product.
- **Uncapped LTE**, if fibre hasn't reached you, you rent, or you want to be
  online this week rather than after an installation.
- **5G**, if you're in a covered suburb and want fibre-like speeds without
  the wait, check the coverage disclaimer honestly, though: it falls back
  to 4G at the edges.
